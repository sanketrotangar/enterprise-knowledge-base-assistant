# Northwind Leave Policy (v1)

This is the previous Northwind Holdings leave policy. It applied to all employees on a Northwind contract in the calendar year before the current revision. It is kept in the knowledge base so HR can answer questions about what people were entitled to last year. It is not the live policy. If a question is about what people get now, use the later revision.

Northwind treated annual leave as a simple accrual. Full-time employees received **fifteen days of annual leave** per calendar year. Part-time employees accrued leave in proportion to contracted hours. The year ran from 1 January to 31 December. New starters accrued from their first day and could take leave once they had built up the days they wanted to use, subject to manager approval. There was no waiting period after probation.

Carry-over was tight. Employees could carry **five unused days** into January of the next year. Those days had to be used by 31 March or they expired. Cash in lieu of unused leave was not offered except when someone left the company and still had a positive balance. Northwind did not run a "use it or lose it" campaign beyond a November reminder from HR.

Sick leave sat next to annual leave but was counted separately. Employees could take up to ten paid sick days a year without a doctor's note for the first three consecutive days. After three days, a note was required. Sick days did not reduce the fifteen-day annual pot. Unpaid time off for longer illness went through HR and was not automatic.

Public holidays were in addition to the fifteen days. Northwind observed the federal holiday calendar of the country where the employee was contracted. If a holiday fell on a weekend, the company did not automatically add a substitute day unless local labour law required it. Religious holidays that were not public holidays could be taken from the annual pot or as unpaid leave.

Parental leave in this version was statutory only. Northwind did not add company-paid weeks on top of the legal minimum. Managers were told not to promise extra weeks in offer letters. Flexible return-to-work plans existed as an informal practice, not as a written right.

Booking leave used the HR portal. Requests needed to go in at least ten working days before the first day of leave for blocks of five days or more. Shorter absences could be agreed with the line manager in writing. Team calendars were supposed to stay green enough that no more than one third of a team was out on the same day, except during the late-December shutdown.

The late-December shutdown was five working days. Those days came out of the fifteen-day pot unless an employee had already used their balance, in which case the shutdown days were unpaid. This was a common source of complaints and is one reason the later revision changed the shutdown treatment.

Volunteer time did not exist in this version. Employees who wanted to spend a day at a school or charity used annual leave or went unpaid. Study leave for job-related courses could be granted at manager discretion, capped at two days a year, and was not an entitlement.

Managers could refuse a request if the dates collided with a known delivery, a month-end close, or a planned audit. A refusal had to name a business reason and offer an alternative window inside thirty days. Employees could ask HR to review a refusal. HR's review looked only at process, not at whether the business reason was "good enough."

This v1 text should not be quoted as current entitlement. It exists so that questions about last year's fifteen-day grant, the five-day carry-over, and the shutdown-from-pot rule can still be answered without mixing them into the live policy.
